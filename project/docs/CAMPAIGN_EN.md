# cub4Studio — US launch plan and English campaign kit

Planning date: September 26, 2026. Proposed experiment, not a forecast. No ads have been published and no spend has been authorized.

## 1. Two separate offers

A — Software: Lead Finder by cub4Studio, for freelancers and small agencies prospecting local businesses. Conversion: qualified beta request, then activated user, then paid subscriber after production readiness.
B — Development service: cub4Studio builds a custom lead finder or business tool for an agency. Conversion: qualified project inquiry. Use the prototype as a demonstration of our work, not evidence of customer revenue.

Start with A to validate product demand. If the priority is immediate service revenue, run B instead; do not divide a small budget across both. Never send people who expect a $19 subscription to a custom-development quote without explaining the offer.

An optional later test can use available web-design or digital-marketing interests, but do not invent unavailable interest names or split a small initial budget.
Placements: eligible Facebook/Instagram placements, with separate 4:5 feed and 9:16 vertical assets checked in previews. Browser extension installs are not required for this web product.
Budget hypothesis: US$15/day for 7 days = US$105 total, only if affordable and separately authorized. With a smaller budget, run fewer days or validate organically first. This experiment can be inconclusive.
Ads: two initial variants, same offer and form; test the hook. Add the third only after evaluating response quality.
CTA button: Learn More if available; on-creative CTA: Request beta access. Do not use Start free search while access is only a beta form.

## 7. Measurement and decisions

Track ad → submitted form → qualified request → beta activation → completed search → export/save → return use → paid subscription. The current MVP does not implement analytics, a signup system or checkout. Instrument these during production work. Count an actual server-side completed action, not just button clicks.

Qualified beta request: a freelancer/agency with an identifiable service, recurring prospecting workflow and willingness to test. Measure cost per qualified request as well as raw CPL. CTR alone is not success.

Review after 48–72 hours for broken links and low-quality requests; avoid declaring a winner from tiny samples. Interview 5–10 relevant users before expanding acquisition. If 20 requests produce no qualified prospects, inspect message and audience. If qualified users do not complete a search, fix onboarding/data before spending more. These are operating heuristics, not statistical thresholds or platform benchmarks.

At US$19, a 5% + US$0.50 transaction fee leaves US$17.55 before provider, hosting, support, tax, refunds and acquisition. CAC ceiling must be based on measured contribution margin and retention. Example only: US$15 contribution/month × 2-month desired payback = US$30 CAC ceiling. If just 10% of qualified requests pay, that implies US$3 per qualified request for that scenario. Replace assumptions with actual cohort data.

## 8. Seven-step launch sequence

1. Test local demo and choose whether to sell software access or development.
2. Activate a provider account, check allowance and obtain first real result.
3. Compare 30 real businesses across 3 searches; inspect fields and provider cost.
4. Record real UI footage, clearly label any demo data.
5. Complete production gates for subscription sales, or prepare a service inquiry form.
6. Invite first testers and document objections, usage and willingness to pay.
7. Run one constrained English campaign after the destination and tracking are ready.
